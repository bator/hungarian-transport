Hungarian transport 1.10.24. Includes fresh Volán and city GTFS indexes (and the MÁV index when built).

## Highlights

- **Signed timetables from GitHub Pages.** The daily MÁV index (`mav-index.json`) and the weekly Volán and city indexes (`timetables.json`) are now signed (ed25519, `.sig` next to each manifest). The integration installs an index from Pages only when its manifest's signature verifies against the key it ships with; a missing or bad signature installs nothing and keeps the index you have. Right after a deploy the integration waits out GitHub Pages' cache once before it calls a mismatch a failure.
- **Release city indexes win by version.** Pages entries name the release they were built against (`release_version`). A Pages city index never replaces the city index of a newer release you just installed; a strictly newer Pages index still installs, and once an operator in both city indexes is dated more than 21 days older in the installed one than on Pages, the usual feed comparison decides again. The Volán index goes by feed date, as before.
- **Why an index was not installed.** `sensor.hungarian_transport_volan_timetable` and `sensor.hungarian_transport_city_timetables` show `pages_reason` (for example `bad_signature`, `unknown_key`, `release_newer`, `mismatch`), `sensor.hungarian_transport_daily_mav_check` shows `reason` (for example `bad_signature`, `mismatch`, `too_large`), and all three show `pages_settling` while a check waits for Pages' cache. Names and values are translated (English, Hungarian). The notification names the feed day of the city timetables.
- **New option "Ask mavplusz.hu"** (on by default). Off, nothing more is sent to mavplusz.hu: trains and buses read only the GTFS-Realtime feeds, and a Budapest board FUTÁR cannot answer falls back to Transitous. A request already sent when you save still gets its answer.
- **Steadier trains from mavplusz.hu.** A trip or route the planner rejects holds back only itself: a large request is split, and a single failing trip waits longer each time it fails again (60 s, then 2, 4, 8 and 15 minutes). A trip with unexpected data fails alone; the others in its request are answered. Only an outage of the trip requests backs off the shared limit: when only the route lists fail, they are retried once a minute with their own warning, and trips and the BKK fallback carry on. The outage warning names what fails (trips or route lists), an outage nobody asks about any more is forgotten after 15 minutes, and after an outage every train is answered again in the first round.
- **BKK boards during a FUTÁR outage.** While FUTÁR is down, the planner is asked first and FUTÁR is probed once every 30 s. A board or trip the planner holds is answered at once, also on the probe, which then asks FUTÁR in the background. Cards asking for the same board at once share one request and one FUTÁR token. A FUTÁR 429 is logged apart from an outage. The log says once per 5 minutes that FUTÁR is down and once that it is back.
- **Safer installs.** A Home Assistant stop or an options save during an install, weekly or daily MÁV, waits for it to finish and records it; a stop during or right after a download keeps the verified files, so the next try downloads only the rest.
- **Sturdier Pages and release pipeline.** A re-run of an old site job cannot put older indexes back on Pages, the daily check turns red when the published MÁV manifest is missing or unreadable, the rebuild after a release builds against that release even when GitHub's release list lags, a release runs the tests in random order and on Home Assistant 2024.10 before it publishes, and the MÁV build falls back to Geofabrik's extract of the days before when its latest one cannot be downloaded.

## Upgrade notes

- Timetables from Pages install only from the signed site; it is live since before this release.
- Right after the update the city timetables sensor may show `pages_result: superseded` with `pages_reason: release_newer` until Pages is rebuilt against 1.10.24 (the release starts the rebuild at once) and the integration's next daily check (06:00 by default, or the first check about 10 minutes after a restart) sees it. That is expected and clears by itself.
